Max-Cut on Erdős–Rényi graphs

The NP-hard Max-Cut problem on a G(50, 0.20) random graph, mapped to an antiferromagnetic Ising Hamiltonian and annealed to a bipartition. Every edge wants its two endpoints on opposite sides — the annealer’s job is to satisfy as many edges as possible at once. Why flipping works

Max-Cut, rewritten as physics. Each node carries a spin si = ±1 — its side of the partition.

H = Σ(i,j) ∈ E si sj
antiferromagnetic Ising Hamiltonian
  • cutOpposite spins — the edge contributes −1. The antiferromagnetic coupling is satisfied.
  • uncutSame spin — the edge contributes +1. Energy goes up, so the annealer tries to flip it away.
  • Minimising H is exactly maximising the cut: H = |E| − 2 × cut. One number, both views.

  3. Heat it up: raise the start temperature and the annealer wanders longer before it settles.
