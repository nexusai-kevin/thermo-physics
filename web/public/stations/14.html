Why a clash costs so much: the Lennard-Jones wall

Two non-bonded atoms follow E(r) = 4ε[(σ/r)12 − (σ/r)6]. Sit a contact at the well minimum (r ≈ 1.12σ) and it is mildly favourable. Push it just 25% closer and the r−12 repulsion wall takes over — that is a steric clash. Rotamer packing never moves atoms continuously; it jumps each side chain between discrete χ1 wells until no contact sits on the wall. Curve drawn from the Lennard-Jones formula; the demo above scores each clashing facing pair with a fixed penalty from this wall.

The three χ1 rotamers

gauche+ · χ1 = −60°
Side chain splayed up-left of the backbone direction — retracted from a facing partner.
trans · χ1 = 180°
Side chain extended straight across the hairpin core. Longest reach — the clash-prone state when the facing residue reaches too.
gauche− · χ1 = +60°
Side chain splayed down-right — the other retracted well. Packing is choosing, per residue, which well to sit in.

Facing pairs across the core clash when their combined reach overlaps: a trans side chain against anything but a fully retracted partner. The Potts sampler scores every facing pair and swivels the offenders.
