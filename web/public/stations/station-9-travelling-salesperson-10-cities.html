How the QUBO holds a tour together

The tour is written as a 10×10 binary matrix: cell (stop, city) is 1 when that city is visited at that stop. A valid tour needs exactly one 1 in every row and every column, so the QUBO adds a heavy penalty for any row or column that breaks the rule, and a cost term that sums the distance of each leg the matrix selects.

Chromatic block Gibbs updates split the ten stop-positions into five colored blocks. A sweep freezes four blocks and resamples the fifth, so row and column constraints are repaired block by block instead of being fought all at once — and because every proposed move is a swap inside the permutation, the matrix never stops being a legal tour while the distance term pulls it shorter.

The demo above runs that process on the cities in front of you. Its optimum is not assumed — it is proven by exhaustive Held–Karp dynamic programming over all orderings of these ten cities, so the gap readout is exact.
