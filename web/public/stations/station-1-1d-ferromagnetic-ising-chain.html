The 1D Ferromagnetic Ising Chain

Sixteen coupled spins with nearest-neighbour ferromagnetic coupling. Hot, the spins fluctuate independently; cooled, they settle into the uniform ground state. Drag the temperature and watch the chain decide — every spin below is simulated live, and you can click any spin to flip it yourself.

Hot — disordered

Thermal energy overwhelms the coupling. Spins flip freely, neighbours ignore each other, magnetisation hovers near zero and the energy stays high.

Cold — the ground state

The coupling wins. Flips that break alignment are rejected, domains merge, and the chain locks into one uniform direction at the exact ground state, E = −2.0. Simulated benchmark result, results may vary (not customer results).

The physics

H = −J Σi si si+1 ,  si = ±1,  J > 0

Each spin wants to point the same way as its two neighbours — that is the ferromagnetic coupling J. A pair that agrees lowers the energy; a pair that disagrees raises it.

  • Sampler: Metropolis — propose a single spin flip, always accept it if it lowers the energy, accept it with probability e−ΔE/T if it raises it.
  • A sweep is 16 flip attempts — one per spin, on average.
  • Ground state: all 16 spins aligned, every bond satisfied. There are two of them: all up, or all down.
  • Why 1D is special: a single domain wall costs the same energy anywhere on the chain, so at any nonzero temperature walls wander freely — which is why this chain orders only as T → 0, and why it makes such a clean first benchmark.
